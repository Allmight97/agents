# Visual Taste

Use this reference when a durable artifact needs a distinct visual register.
Choose one coherent system from the subject matter; do not assemble a collage
of fashionable effects.

## Art Direction Brief

Before styling, name five decisions:

1. **Domain metaphor:** the real-world surface that fits the material, such as
   a field guide, patch board, signal console, lab notebook, or service manual.
2. **Structural form:** the relationship that should dominate, such as lanes,
   a matrix, a timeline, a tree, or a control panel.
3. **Type roles:** one display or reading face, one interface face, and
   monospace only where machine state or evidence benefits.
4. **Color semantics:** a small neutral field plus accents with stable meanings
   such as verified, uncertain, blocked, or selected.
5. **Surface behavior:** hard rules, paper fields, console panels, editorial
   whitespace, or another treatment that belongs to the metaphor.

Let hierarchy come from typography, spacing, alignment, and rules. Use panels
only for real information units. Vary section rhythm when the relationships
change. Ornament should identify the subject, encode state, or improve
navigation.

## Source-Derived Examples

These examples were distilled from successful generated onboarding artifacts.
They describe transferable mechanics rather than reusable templates.

### Patch-Replay Repository As Engineering Field Guide

- Dark technical-paper field with hard horizontal rules and square edges.
- Large sans-serif project identity; serif explanatory copy; monospace evidence
  and status labels.
- A restrained spacecraft mark anchors the subject without turning the page
  into a product landing screen.
- Build, runtime, and proof appear as separate lanes that visibly converge.
- Muted blue identifies structure, green verified state, amber drift, and rose
  open risk.
- Tables, marginal notes, and proof ledgers replace interchangeable card grids.

### Local Dictation App As Signal Console

- Near-black console field with a persistent navigation rail and a compact
  waveform mark.
- Large literal title, then the microphone-to-paste path as the primary visual.
- Sans-serif interface copy and monospace machine labels keep the surface
  technical without mimicking a terminal.
- Cyan, amber, violet, and coral encode verified, intent, optional, and boundary
  states rather than decorating sections.
- Pipeline nodes, ownership rows, and expandable evidence carry detail; the
  opening avoids a feature-marketing layout.

## Taste Check

Before delivery, verify:

- the first screen names and explains the subject literally before using a
  tagline, metaphor, or compressed claim;
- the opening cannot be read literally as a materially different product,
  prerequisite, state, or scope;
- the visual language is specific to the subject and consistent across the
  artifact;
- repeated cards, pills, gradients, glass effects, and oversized slogans have
  been replaced by a clearer subject-specific structure when they do not encode
  meaning;
- section shapes change only when the information relationship changes;
- removing an ornamental element would reduce recognition, state clarity, or
  navigation—not merely visual novelty.
